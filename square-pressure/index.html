How counting works
  • A square is attacked if the piece could capture on it. Your own pieces included — that is a defence, and it counts.
  • Pawns attack the two diagonal squares in front of them. A pawn push is not an attack, and an occupied diagonal still counts.
  • Sliders stop at the first piece in the way: a rook on d1 behind a rook on d3 attacks d3 and nothing past it. No x-rays.
  • Pins are irrelevant. A pinned knight still attacks everything it could jump to.
  • The king attacks all eight neighbours, legal or not.
  • En passant and castling are ignored. Positions are built from real pawn structures, so the piece placement is the kind you will actually meet.
Positions are generated from named pawn skeletons — Najdorf, Carlsbad, Mar del Plata, hedgehog, four-against-three — with pieces sampled from the squares they usually occupy in that structure. Nothing here is a puzzle to solve; it is a board to see.
